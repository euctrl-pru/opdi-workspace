# Link flight pieces only where stops cannot fit

Nobody publishes a validated method for giving one flight one identity across hours-long ADS-B holes, so OPDI would be building and measuring this itself, not adopting an established rule. Public practice splits on a time gap of 10 to 30 minutes and mostly stops there. The only published aviation plausibility tests the notes found are OpenSky's 2021 constant-extrapolation rejoin and GAIA's split rules, and neither reports a split or link error rate. The multi-target association literature mostly addresses a problem OPDI lacks: `icao24` persists, so the question is not "which piece continues which" but "did this airframe land inside this hole", which is a per-gap classification computable with `lag` over an airframe window. Implied speed alone cannot answer it. At OPDI's median hole of about 191 minutes, a global 315 kt floor misses any stop costing under about 66 minutes, which includes a quick turnaround (arithmetic in this report, from unvalidated inputs). The stronger candidate is a time-budget test with geography: link only when no plausibly dark airport fits inside the ellipse that the gap's time allows, and otherwise leave the pieces apart. The hard evidence on failure rates does not exist yet in this domain, and the design that would produce it, withheld data around real turnarounds as negative controls, appears in none of the sources read. OPDI's Network Manager and APDF truth makes that measurement possible for Europe, and it should come before any production arm.

**How to read the evidence.** Tags follow the notes. **[read]**, **[paper text]** and **[source code]** mean a note author read the primary text. **[snippet]**, **[excerpt]**, **[search extract]** and **[abstract only]** mean the figure rests on a search summary or abstract and is unverified, so it is reported here as a lead and not as a finding. **[local]** means this repository; I re-checked the `flicker_2026.csv` counts, the `base.py` values and the two paper passages quoted from `track-construction-v1` and `adsc-fusion-v1`. **[note-derived]** marks a note author's arithmetic or judgement call, and **[this report]** marks mine. Neither of those is a published result. The 1,204-of-1,334 (90%), 191-minute and edge-altitude figures come from the brief, and I did not re-derive them.

## Public practice splits on time and almost never rejoins

Three designs recur, none justified by measured linking accuracy. **Time-only:** `traffic` groups on `(icao24, callsign)` and splits at 10 minutes with no altitude, position or speed test, though a `condition(f1, f2)` callback lets a caller veto a split **[source code]** ([flight.py](https://github.com/xoolive/traffic/blob/master/src/traffic/core/flight.py)). Sun's companion code uses DBSCAN with a 30-minute scale over scaled time and altitude **[source code]** ([flightextract.py](https://github.com/junzis/flight-data-processor/blob/master/flightextract.py)). **Time plus a physics rejoin:** OpenSky's 2021 dataset paper ends a track after 10 minutes out of coverage but merges pieces if a constant extrapolation fits within 0.5 degrees, and otherwise assumes a landing outside coverage **[paper text]** ([ESSD 2021](https://essd.copernicus.org/articles/13/357/2021/)). At a 4,000 km hole that tolerance is under 2% of the distance, so an ocean crossing probably stays in two pieces **[note-derived]**, and whether production tables still use this rule is undocumented. **Vertical-profile evidence:** readsb never starts a new leg on a time gap alone. It requires a major descent and then ground contact or a major climb. Its gap rule asks for a climb more than 12 minutes after the descent, a reception gap over 5 minutes, and a post-gap sample on the ground, with invalid baro altitude, or below 20,000 ft **[source code]** ([globe_index.c](https://github.com/wiedehopf/readsb/blob/dev/globe_index.c)). It publishes no accuracy figure, and its time/distance rule is disabled in the code.

**GAIA is the most complete published rule set, and it is the closest thing to a hidden-turnaround test** ([supplement](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/24/725/2024/acp-24-725-2024-supplement.pdf)) **[paper text]**. It groups by ICAO address, sub-groups on callsign, type, origin-destination pair and tail number, and splits where segment length exceeds the origin-destination distance. It also splits where `dt` exceeds great-circle travel time at 180 m/s for jets or 70 m/s for props (6 h with no airport data), or where waypoints sit below 10,000 ft during cruise. A 10-minute "minimum aircraft turnaround time" separates a diversion candidate from a new flight. The 180 m/s bound works out to about 350 kt **[this report]**; the note's summary does not settle exactly which distance it applies to. GAIA reports rejection rates of 3.3-6.6%, and no split or link accuracy.

The remaining evidence is thin. The 2025 OpenSky report splits ADS-C into flights by callsign and then pulls ADS-B by transponder code, with no time, altitude or position gate and only qualitative verification **[paper text]** ([arXiv 2505.06254](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.06254v1)). Its claim of accuracy improving "by up to 40%" has no stated metric or baseline in the text the notes extracted, so it should not be used. Sun et al. (2023) merge Spire and OpenSky, select by a mid-Atlantic band and keep flights with data below 6,000 ft, yielding about 200 complete flights in one day **[paper text]** ([SIDs 2023](https://www.sesarju.eu/sites/default/files/documents/sid/2023/Papers/SIDs_2023_paper_73%20final.pdf)). That is a survivorship filter, and it says nothing about the flights dropped **[note-derived]**. Commercial trackers carry flights through oceans but do not publish their identity logic. Flightradar24's 240-minute estimates for known routes against about 10 minutes without a known destination suggest route data sustains identity, though that rests on a **[search extract]** and an inference ([FR24 blog](https://www.flightradar24.com/blog/inside-flightradar24/exploring-estimated-coverage-in-flightradar24/)). PRC winners did not face the problem at all, because organisers supplied `flight_id`.

**Schedule, flight-plan and rotation linking has no accuracy evidence behind it.** Two targeted searches found no evaluated rotation-based linker, and the note warns this may be a terminology miss (tail assignment), not proof of absence. GAIA's commercial metadata gives origin-destination codes for 79% of flights and fleet data for 59% **[paper text]** ([ACP 2024](https://acp.copernicus.org/articles/24/725/2024/)), and those figures measure availability, not correctness. Open route databases have no accuracy evaluation in anything found, and OpenFlights routes reportedly stopped updating in 2014 **[search extract]** ([FAQ](https://nginx.openflights.org/faq)). A schedule's error rate would become the linker's error rate **[note-derived]**. I would keep Network Manager and APDF out of the runtime rule altogether: they are European, the repository forbids querying the warehouse from the pipeline, and a rule fed by the validation source cannot be validated against it **[this report]**.

Two local corrections follow. The Sun parameters that one note calls unconfirmed are settled by another note's code reading. And `CLAUDE.md`'s "gap segmentation at 5 min" as a PRC finding looks unsupported: the note found no such split in either winner's active path, and my grep of `resourceful-quiver` `.py` and `.ipynb` files finds `assign_clusters` only at its definition. The PRC negative result on completing trajectories does not bear on assigning one identity while leaving the hole empty **[note-derived]**, and its authors concede their heuristic may be at fault **[paper text]**.

## A persistent address turns association into classifying each gap

Track-segment association, min-cost flow, multiple-hypothesis tracking and MCMC data association all resolve competition among many targets for the same fragments. TSA predicts old segments and retrodicts new ones to a common time, then solves a 2-D assignment; its results come from dense simulation at scan scale **[abstract only]** ([Yeom 2003](https://experts.mcmaster.ca/scholarly-works/144032), [Raghu 2018](https://www.amrita.edu/publication/comprehensive-track-segment-association-for-improved-track-continuity/)). Non-overlapping pieces of one airframe form a path, since linking p1 to p3 while excluding p2 would put the aircraft in another flight during its own. So K pieces have K−1 independent boundaries, and with independent scores the optimum is a threshold on each boundary **[note-derived, and I agree]**. Dynamic programming (optimal partitioning, of which PELT is a pruned form) earns its cost only for whole-flight constraints such as exactly one take-off and one landing. That needs a per-group function, which `CLAUDE.md` treats as a deliberate architectural step. Start without it.

What transfers is the warning about the trade-off, and gating's limits. In Zhang's CVPR 2008 tracker, adding an explicit occlusion model took fragments from **58 to 20 while ID switches rose from 7 to 15** **[read]**, and the authors state that large temporal gaps raise the chance of errant association ([Zhang 2008](https://www.cvlibs.net/projects/autonomous_vision_survey/literature/Zhang2008CVPR.pdf)). This is pedestrian video, so only the direction carries over. Reachability gates decay fast: at 500 kt over 6 hours the bound is 3,000 NM, which passes nearly any pair, and under white-noise acceleration position uncertainty at 6 hours is 41.6 times that at 30 minutes whatever the noise level **[note-derived]**. A bare Mahalanobis gate gets worse, because growing covariance makes wrong pairs look better at longer gaps **[note-derived, follows from the formula; no paper states it for long gaps]**. Kinematic gates therefore reject the impossible at long gaps and cannot confirm the possible. Over-the-horizon radar, an apparent analogue, solves a different problem: simultaneous multipath tracks of one target **[search summary]** ([Bogner 1998](https://elmi.hbku.edu.qa/en/publications/association-of-tracks-from-over-the-horizon-radar/)).

**Learned linking has not shown benefit over good rules for motion-only, long-gap problems.** SUSHI's strong video scores depend mainly on appearance embeddings, which aircraft lack ([Cetintas 2023](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.03038)). Quo Vadis is the honest test: from a baseline HOTA of 50.71, a bird's-eye Kalman filter reached 54.11 and a learned forecaster 54.52, while the most accurate forecaster scored lower as a tracker (53.81), with gaps of seconds and pedestrians **[read, tables partly garbled in extraction]** ([Dendorfer 2022](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2210.07681)). TSA-cTFER reports 94.59% on interrupted radar TSA, from keyword extraction only, with unknown gap lengths ([Cao 2024](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/remotesensing/remotesensing-16-03380/article_deploy/remotesensing-16-03380.pdf)). AIS-ITD-US, the nearest long-gap benchmark at up to 180 minutes, deliberately replaces vessel identity with synthetic IDs, so it tests a harder and different task ([IEEE DataPort](https://ieee-dataport.org/documents/ais-itd-us)). What OPDI has and these lack is labels, so a shallow classifier over edge features is the defensible first learned step, after rules set the baseline **[note-derived]**.

## Maritime work supplies design patterns, and no thresholds

AIS gap cut-offs span minutes to 48 hours with little validated justification, and the speed caps run 15 to 50 kn. Global Fishing Watch's code uses 8 h and 25 kn while its README still says 24 h and 30 kn **[read]**, so the code is the one to cite ([core.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/GlobalFishingWatch/gpsdio-segment/master/gpsdio_segment/core.py)). Four patterns carry over. **Ambiguity breaks the link:** if several segments score within a factor of 10 of the best, GFW closes them and starts anew **[read]**. An unnecessary split is visible and recoverable while a wrong merge is silent **[note-derived]**. **Separate "a gap occurred" from "the trip ended":** Patroumpas et al. use a shorter gap-event threshold and a longer trip break, because a vessel can lose contact while still heading to its destination **[read]** ([arXiv 2603.12287](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.12287)). **Split, then rejoin on a physical test:** as in GFW's segmenter and, per secondary description, Zhao et al. ([Paulig & Okhrin](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.04402)). **A gap is informative only where the receiver would have heard the target:** Welch et al. require a gap of at least 12 h, more than 50 nm offshore, predicted reception above 10 positions a day and 14 positions in the prior 12 h, and report **3.72% false positives and 0.86 precision against labelled events** **[read]** ([Welch 2022](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.abq2109)). The implied recall of about 0.47 is **[note-derived]** from the reported F0.5 of 0.739 and is not a published number.

The hidden-stop question is open at sea too. The notes found **no per-event precision or recall for port calls inferred from gaps**, and the low-speed signal appears only in a personal developer page **[excerpt]**. GFW counts a gap over 4 hours in port as stop evidence but labels those visits confidence 2 that "require a further inspection" **[read]** ([GFW caveats](https://globalfishingwatch.org/our-apis/documentation/docs/v3/general-api-doc/data-caveats)). The GFW FAQ's account of false confidence-4 visits, created when a vessel moved for over 4 hours near anchorages without stopping and removed in 2024, is only an **[excerpt]** ([FAQ](https://globalfishingwatch.org/faqs/port-visit-data-updates/)). Aggregate agreement, such as Hütten's within 20% of proprietary-data studies ([arXiv 2511.23016](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.23016)) **[read]**, can hide false links and false splits cancelling each other **[note-derived]**. Paulig & Okhrin state that "no definitive ground truth" exists **[read]**, which is the position OPDI is not in.

The thresholds do not transfer because the time ratio inverts. GFW's bound allows 200 nm of movement, which a jet covers in under 30 minutes, and a port stay is long relative to the gap while a turnaround is short relative to an ocean crossing **[note-derived]**. Aircraft, in compensation, have evidence that ships lack: a hidden landing requires a descent and a climb, and any reading of those at the hole's edges has no maritime equivalent. The AIS literature therefore offers no values for what should be OPDI's primary feature.

## At the median hole, speed floors miss quick stops and geography decides

**A 191-minute hole is about 1,530 NM at 480 kt, slightly beyond the note's geometric estimate of 700-1,400 NM (1.5-3 h)**, which assumed a radio-horizon gap between Ireland and Newfoundland **[note-derived; conversion this report]**. No source publishes a gap-length distribution for any region, so OPDI's own figure is the best data available. The population it comes from is selected on ADS-C equipage, so it should not be read as representative of other airframes. Hole edges almost never below 5,000 ft also mean that the current 15-minute low-altitude branch and readsb-style triggers rarely fire on these holes. The edge altitudes say the aircraft was cruising at both moments, and little more once the gap exceeds about an hour.

**Implied speed depends on the stop's total cost.** For a fixed gap, a stop is detectable only if it costs more than T_gap × (1 − f), where f is the floor as a share of cruise speed **[this report, rearranging the note's formula]**. At 191 minutes and 480 kt:

| Hidden stop cost (turnaround + 20 min penalty) | Implied speed | Global 315 kt floor (detects ≥ 66 min) | Per-flight 0.85 floor (detects ≥ 29 min) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 45 min (25 min low-cost turn) | 367 kt | Missed | Caught, unless wind masks it |
| 79 min (58.6 min medium mean) | 281 kt | Caught | Caught |
| 87 min (67 min heavy "low") | 261 kt | Caught | Caught |
| 154 min (134.3 min heavy mean) | 93 kt | Caught | Caught |

Every input to this table is soft. The turnaround values are **[snippet]** from a EUROCONTROL page that returned 403, and the note flags the low/base/high row as ambiguously labelled ([Standard Inputs](https://ansperformance.eu/economics/cba/standard-inputs/v9.0.3/chapters/turnaround_time.html)). The 25-minute value comes from low-authority pages **[snippet]**, and the figure OAG publishes directly is a mean, 59-77 minutes actual across US majors ([OAG](https://www.oag.com/blog/science-aircraft-turnarounds)). The 315 kt floor and 20-minute penalty are assumptions **[note-derived]**, and the 0.85 ratio is explicitly **unvalidated**. It also fails in a specific way: a tailwind about 54 kt stronger inside the hole than at the edges would lift a 45-minute stop back above the floor **[this report]**, and the reported typical jet of about 96 kt is itself a **[snippet]** ([EarthDate](https://earthdate.org/node/457)). The upper bound is more solid, since reported ground-speed peaks of about 705-726 kt **[snippet]** are instantaneous, so about 750 kt over a whole gap signals an identity error rather than wind **[note-derived]**. Its role is detecting duplicate addresses, not separating flights.

**Time slack and airport reachability are the same test, and the second is stronger.** Slack is T_gap − D / V_fast. A stop of cost T_stop fits only if slack ≥ T_stop, which is exactly the condition for the note's ellipse (foci at the edges, major axis V_fast × (T_gap − T_stop)) to exist at all **[this report]**. So slack is the cheap pre-filter, and the airport list adds geography **[note-derived for both tests]**. At the median hole the verdict depends on V_fast: at 650 kt slack is 50 minutes and a stop is not excluded, while at 572 kt slack is 31 minutes and a 35-minute minimum stop is excluded **[this report]**. Even at 650 kt with a 45-minute stop, the ellipse is only about 200 NM half-width and extends about 27 NM beyond each focus, so coastal airports near the edges fall outside it and few mid-ocean candidates remain **[this report]**. This is where the test is strong on the North Atlantic. Over Africa or Siberia airports are denser and edges occur at any phase, so a rule tuned on the NAT should not be assumed to carry over **[note-derived]**.

Two refinements come from applying Welch's reception logic **[this report; untested, found in no source]**. First, an airport where OPDI routinely sees landings at that hour cannot host a hidden stop unless receivers failed, so the ellipse should be tested against dark airports only, derived from OPDI's own landing counts. Second, ADS-C can serve as evidence without merging rows. A hole containing cruise-level ADS-C samples spaced closer than the minimum stop time has effectively ruled out a landing, and this needs no new `source` column and no `track_id` boundary. The census cadence of 277-1,139 s **[local]** makes this plausible. ADS-C rarely shows the landing itself (1-67 rows a day below 10,000 ft, `on_ground` never true **[local]**), so absence of ADS-C evidence says nothing. The same structure gives a real-hole positive set, limited to equipped aircraft.

**Callsign agreement is a veto, not a confirmation.** Same-number legs on one day exist, though sources disagree on how common they are, and all of it is **[snippet]** or anecdotal ([ACI](https://aci-standards.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/ACIDD/pages/17695395/Callsign), [Bellingcat](https://bellingcat.com/resources/how-tos/2019/10/15/a-beginners-guide-to-flight-tracking)). On 2026-06-01, 10,241 of 12,440 callsign boundaries traced to flicker and 2,199 were genuine **[local]**, so edges must use the debounced value. The 42.4% of `legacy` tracks with no callsign **[local]** is a warning about blanks, though it describes the old rule's grouping and not A9's data. A blank should be neutral, not a mismatch.

|---|---|---|---|
| On-route technical stop, same callsign | Long distance, small speed change, cruise-looking edges | **False join**, the main risk | Reasoning only; no rate found |

## Candidate rule: vetoes first, positive evidence required, unresolved means apart

The rule below is a set of starting values to calibrate, and no row is an established threshold. Vetoes apply first, then a link needs at least one piece of positive evidence, and everything else stays split with its gap annotated. Thresholds go in aviation units with the unit in the field name, and edge speeds convert at the point of use, as `CLAUDE.md` requires.

| Criterion | Starting value | Status | Main failure mode |
|---|---|---|---|
| Veto: placeholder address | Shortlist, plus same address at two separated positions | Examples **[excerpt]**; list must be measured | Unlisted duplicates |
| Veto: time overlap | `gap_min` < 0 | Structural **[note-derived]** | Rare; quarantine, do not resolve |
| Veto: implied speed | > 750 kt, with a minimum gap and distance | Peaks **[snippet]**; bound **[note-derived]** | Timestamp error on short gaps |
| Veto: debounced callsign differs | Both non-blank and different | A9 evidence **[local]** | Late-running suffixes **[snippet]**; never compare ADS-C `flight` (IATA) with ICAO callsign **[local]** |
| Veto: profile | Descending at edge A and climbing at edge B | readsb and GAIA analogues **[source code, paper text]**; thresholds unmeasured | ATC-directed level changes |
| Veto: low edges in an aerodrome zone | Height above field elevation, plus speed | A5 logic **[local]**; low edges are also where reception fails **[note-derived]** | Rejects real flights at coverage edges |
| Evidence A: short cruise gap | Both edges ≥ FL250-300, gap < about 60 min | Stop needs about 65 min, assumed **[note-derived, judgement]** | Military and training flights |
| Evidence B: no room for a stop | Slack < `link_min_stop_min`, about 35 min | **[note-derived]**; calibrate from APDF block times | Sub-25 min stops; too-low V_fast |
| Evidence C: no dark airport in ellipse | V_fast 650 kt or 1.1 × edge speed, T_stop about 45 min | Geometry **[note-derived]**; dark-airport filter **[this report]** | Incomplete airport list; remote strips |
| Evidence D: ADS-C bridge | In-hole cruise samples spaced below minimum stop time | **[this report]** | Equipped aircraft only |
| Secondary: per-flight speed floor | Implied ≥ 0.85 × edge speed | **Unvalidated**; use as an extra veto first, not as evidence | Wind inside the hole |
| Post-check: total elapsed | ≤ about 20 h (about 23 h once Sydney-London flies) | Route figures **[snippet]**; bound **[note-derived]** | Coarse; per-type range is sharper |
| Long-gap policy | Above 3-6 h with no evidence B-D: no link | **Judgement call [note-derived]**, set from negative controls | Lower coverage of the longest holes, and the median sits near this boundary |

The form fits the codebase convention. On a per-piece summary frame, in sketch:

```python
w = Window.partitionBy("icao24").orderBy("t_start")
g = (pieces
  .withColumn("gap_min", (col("t_start") - lag("t_end").over(w)) / 60)
  .withColumn("gc_nm", haversine_nm(lag("lat_last").over(w), lag("lon_last").over(w),
                                    col("lat_first"), col("lon_first")))
  .withColumn("v_implied_kt", col("gc_nm") / (col("gap_min") / 60))
  .withColumn("slack_min", col("gap_min") - col("gc_nm") / V_FAST_KT * 60))
is_link = coalesce(no_veto & (cruise_short | no_stop_fits | no_dark_airport | adsc_bridge), lit(False))
flights = (g.withColumn("is_link", is_link)
            .withColumn("flight_seq", sum((~col("is_link")).cast("int")).over(w)))
```

The `coalesce` makes a NULL feature fail closed, which is the GFW principle expressed in three-valued logic **[this report]**. Evidence C is a broadcast join of candidate gaps against airports, which stays native Spark and runs only on the small candidate set. The elapsed-time post-check is a window over `flight_seq`. Edge values should summarise the last few minutes of cleaned data, not one sample.

**Publish the link as an additive column, not a change to `track_id`.** The notes propose this on identity grounds, since `track_id` boundaries are silent to joiners **[note-derived]**. There is a stronger reason in the repository. `adsc-fusion-v1` records that `segment_id` "is read by **no detector**" and that detectors "already interpolate across thirty-minute ADS-B holes" **[local]**, so one `track_id` across a 3-hour hole would let event detection interpolate across it. A separate `flight_id` with `link_rule_version` and a `link_reason` gives consumers ADEP from the first piece and ADES from the last, while leaving `track_id`, events and published versions untouched **[this report]**. Promoting it into a segmentation arm would be a further boundary and a separate decision. The `link_reason` also gives a graded confidence, on the model of GFW's port-visit levels.

## Validate with hidden turnarounds as negative controls, or false joins stay unmeasurable

**Deleting data from continuous flights tests only one side.** A gap cut from a continuous trip is by construction a true link, so such designs contain no negatives **[note-derived, from a review of AIS validation]**. Sandia's student posters, the nearest aviation precedent, remove data and score stitching, and MITRE lists "segment fusion across gaps" as missing, but both are **[snippet]** and neither's numbers were readable ([Sandia 2023](https://datamine.purdue.edu/posters/Symposium2023_Poster_Sandia_Flight_PDF.pdf), [MITRE](https://www.mitre.org/sites/default/files/publications/pr-17-3649-geospatial-data-threaded_track.pdf)). The design below is the note authors' proposal, and two notes independently failed to find it in the literature, so it is a proposal and not a borrowed standard.

The **positive control** deletes the mid-section between two cruise points of flights confirmed by reference ADEP and ADES, stratified by gap length (15, 30, 60, 120, 180, 360 min), direction and aircraft class. The **negative control** takes consecutive flights of one `icao24` confirmed by block times and deletes the end of flight 1 and the start of flight 2, so that the landing and turnaround fall in the gap, varying the removal so that cruise-level edges appear. Its hard strata are same-callsign pairs and short turns. Edge locations and altitudes should mimic the census of real holes, because deleting data is cleaner than real loss. The **real-hole check** compares ADEP on the first piece and ADES on the last with the flight plan, where a second matching plan or an intermediate ADES identifies a false join. Ristani's definitions fit directly: a fragmentation splits one true identity, and a merge joins two ([Ristani 2016](https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.01775)) **[read]**. Report merges and fragments separately per stratum, since a combined score hides the trade being decided, and do not lean on MOTA or HOTA.

Sample size and prevalence are the arithmetic to respect. Zero false joins among n negatives bounds the rate at about 3/n, so a claim under 0.1% needs **3,000 negatives in that stratum**, and same-callsign short turns may need months of data **[note-derived; the months estimate this report]**. Balanced controls overstate precision, so estimate the true share of hidden stops among real candidates from the real-hole check and report expected false-joined flights per day. The scale reference is the 30,961 boundaries from gaps over 30 minutes on 2026-06-01 **[local]**. The note's "about 310 corrupted pairs per 1%" is an upper bound **[note-derived]**, since many of those are real turnarounds that the vetoes would remove.

Four traps deserve attention **[this report unless tagged]**. Joining truth on `AIRCRAFT_ADDRESS` + callsign + date is circular for callsign evaluation and biases labels against blank or garbled pieces, so match on address and time containment when scoring the callsign veto. A date key also risks missing flights across UTC midnight, which long-haul traffic crosses routinely. Truth is European, so a stop outside Network Manager scope appears as ADES disagreement and not as a second flight, and transfer to Africa or the Pacific stays unmeasured; a region-disjoint split in AIS raised one model's one-hour error from 2.2 km to 24.6 km, a warning from a different task **[read]** ([arXiv 2609.25827](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25827)). And validation must fix one segmentation arm and state it, since joins across `track_id` boundaries return empty without error **[local]**. Downstream, confirm that no linked flight contains an interior landing and that ADEP/ADES agreement improves. Hütten's bracketing by parameter set is a cheap way to report systematic uncertainty **[read]**.

A sensible order is a ladder of named rule versions, in the style of `track-construction-v1`: first the short cruise gap with vetoes, then slack, then the ellipse, then dark airports and ADS-C, and last any per-flight floor. Each rung should be kept only if negative controls justify it. Before any of that, the notes name measurements that replace their weakest assumptions: gap length and edge altitude by region and month, measured minimum turnaround from APDF, the share of same-day same-callsign same-airframe legs, the NAT ground-speed distribution from ADS-C, and duplicate-address rate.

## Conclusion

The research changes where the difficulty sits. The association literature suggests the hard part is deciding which piece continues which, but with `icao24` that is given, and what remains is an inference about an event for which no data exists, at a point where the only available evidence is at the edges. That also changes which test matters: speed plausibility, the literature's standard tool, weakens as gaps grow, while a time budget combined with OPDI's own map of where it can and cannot hear landings becomes more decisive over oceans. No source tests that combination, so it is the most promising and least proven idea here.

The asymmetry should set the default. A false join silently drops a landing and a take-off and pairs the wrong ADEP and ADES, while a missed link leaves two truthful pieces (the weighting is a project decision, not a literature result). An additive, versioned identifier that fails closed means early rules can be conservative and still useful, and each widening can be argued from measured false joins. If OPDI runs the negative-control campaign, it would likely hold the first published false-join figures for same-address flight linking, which makes the benchmark worth publishing whichever rule wins.
